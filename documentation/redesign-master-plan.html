A

Executive concept

The one-paragraph version.

Reposition DMF from a conventional “architectural & engineering firm” into the intelligence layer beneath major development — the practice that plans the land, moves the traffic, lays the infrastructure and engineers the structures that make a place work. The new site behaves like an engineering environment: a dark, precise, cinematic interface where real DMF projects and engineering data are the hero, a persistent “systems” spine (Plan → Move → Build → Sustain) organises the journey, and controlled scroll-driven motion turns technical capability into a story. It borrows Al Faris’s principles — scale, credibility-through-proof, sector clarity, cinematic confidence — without borrowing its identity, and deliberately avoids looking like a heavy-equipment brand.

The standard every page is measured against

“These are the people who understand what has to happen beneath the development for the development to work.”

02

What makes Al Faris work — and how DMF reinterprets it

Studied as a UX strategist, not copied. Al Faris is a dark, industrial, red-accented heavy-equipment brand (near-black grounds, vivid red, neo-grotesque sans, fleet imagery). We extract the why, then translate it to an A&E / infrastructure consultancy.

Hard guardrail

DMF is not a crane company. We borrow Al Faris’s principles of scale, credibility and cinematic confidence — never its branding, colours-as-identity, typography, imagery or layout. The visual language must read infrastructure · masterplanning · engineering systems, not heavy equipment.

Al Faris principle

Establishes scale instantly — huge fleet numbers, tonnage, project counts front-and-centre.

DMF reinterpretation

Lead with engineered data: 50 projects, 7 disciplines, UAE+GCC reach — animated as a technical readout, not a boast banner.

Al Faris principle

Cinematic dark canvas + a single hot accent = authority and focus.

DMF reinterpretation

A dark “engineering environment” with one disciplined DMF accent — but tuned to DMF’s own red, not Al Faris’s, on a graphite (not black) ground.

Al Faris principle

Clear service & sector architecture — the visitor always knows what’s offered and for whom.

DMF reinterpretation

Organise around the development lifecycle (Plan → Move → Build → Sustain), so DMF reads as the specialist who thinks in systems.

Al Faris principle

Credibility through proof — projects, clients, real jobs, real equipment.

DMF reinterpretation

Turn each project into a legible “how it works / what it unlocks” story with scope, disciplines and location — evidence over adjectives.

Al Faris principle

Section transitions create a feeling of a large, capable organisation.

DMF reinterpretation

Scroll-driven reveals and a persistent systems spine make DMF feel like Dubai’s infrastructure intelligence layer — modern, not corporate-heavy.

The shared convention to avoid

Everyone looks the same

Glossy building photography on white, a generalist “expertise × sectors” mega-menu, static big-number banners, architecture-first identity, and copy that floats toward “shaping the world.” Swap the logos and you can’t tell them apart. Motion is the lowest-scoring axis across the entire field.

DMF’s open ground

Infrastructure that moves like a design studio

Parsons owns infrastructure but not craft. Cracknell owns craft but not infrastructure. No one owns both. DMF can be the first infra consultancy that looks and moves like a boutique studio — leading with the ground plane, sections, alignments and networks: the layer before the render.

05

Three creative directions

One recommended. All three are DMF-specific, none look like a template or a crane company.

Direction 01 · Recommended

The Systems Layer

Recommend

A dark, precise engineering environment. Graphite ground, one disciplined DMF-red accent, mono technical labels over a strong neo-grotesque display. A cinematic infrastructure hero, a persistent systems spine, blueprint/CAD line motifs, and scroll-driven diagrams that build a masterplan from the ground up. Reinterprets Al Faris’s dark authority for infrastructure — and lands squarely in the “dark + motion + systems” whitespace no competitor owns.

Best for: premium credibility + the modern-infra lane · Risk: needs disciplined motion to avoid heaviness
Direction 02

Blueprint / Drawn-to-Built

A lighter, technical world: near-white “drawing sheet” ground, fine graph-paper grid, ink/blueprint-blue linework, CAD-style annotations. Engineering drawings animate into finished photography (drawn → built). Editorial and distinctive; the most literal expression of “the layer before the render.”

Best for: differentiation & craft · Risk: less of the dark “premium punch” you responded to
Direction 03

Earth & Substrate

Owns “the roots of development” literally — a warm palette of soil, substrate and alignment-line accents, boutique-editorial layout. The most differentiated from the whole competitor set, furthest from Al Faris. Confident and human, less overtly technical.

Best for: brand distinctiveness · Risk: reads less “engineering”, more “studio”

UI direction & design system

Direction 01 spec. Every page derives from these tokens. (This document is rendered in the proposed language.)

Ground
#0E0F12 graphite#1B1E23 panel#2A2E36 line
Graphite, not pure black — softer, more premium than the benchmark.
Accent
#E23D2C DMF red#B32619 deep
Exact accent is a decision (see §12) — tuned to DMF’s own red, deliberately distinct from Al Faris. One accent only; used with restraint.
Text
#EAECEF primary · #9BA1AA muted · #6B7079 faint — all ≥ AA on graphite.
Display type
Tight neo-grotesque, heavy weight, negative tracking. To be licensed/self-hosted (e.g. a grotesque in the Suisse/Neue-Haas family) — no serif; not Al Faris’s Univers.
Utility type
Monospace for labels, metadata and data readouts — the “engineered” texture that carries the technical identity.
Body type
Clean humanist/grotesque sans; ~65-char measure; strict type scale.
Grid & space
12-col, generous gutters, 8px spacing base, blueprint hairline grid used as texture in bands.
Components
Metadata panels (mono label + value), spec-sheet cards, discipline chips, filter pills, project explorer, systems diagram, regional map.
Glass
Used sparingly & architecturally — transparent nav, one or two floating data panels over video. Never a gimmick.
Imagery
Real DMF projects. Consistent crops/ratios; a drawn-to-built motif; graphite duotone option for cohesion.
10

Experience systems

Motion, video, projects, expertise and mobile — each engineered, each with a graceful fallback.

G · Motion

Engineered, not playful

One easing family, defined durations, reveal-on-scroll, number counters, blur-to-sharp, pinned systems diagram. Every animation communicates something. The site must look excellent with motion disabled.

H · Video

Cinematic but safe

Desktop hero video + mobile-lite alt + poster image + lazy load + muted autoplay + reduced-motion still. No blocking load; graceful fallback if video fails. Real footage preferred; placeholders clearly marked.

I · Projects

The core experience

Scroll expands image → name → location/discipline → technical metadata → detail. Filter by discipline & country; regional map cross-links. Each project = evidence.

Expertise

A systems diagram, not a list

Plan→Move→Build→Sustain

Show the relationship between the 7 disciplines, not just their names.

J · Mobile

Designed, not collapsed

Simplified nav, vertical storytelling, touch-first controls, reduced animation, mobile video, legible transparent panels, thumb-friendly CTAs. Performance-first.

Partnerships

Credibility graph

partner → project → DMF role → disciplines. Only verified relationships shown; the ecosystem around DMF made legible.

Technical, content & assets

What powers it — and the honest list of what only DMF can confirm.

K · Architecture

Evolve the current stack

Next.js (App Router) · Tailwind · GSAP + ScrollTrigger · Lenis. Add Framer Motion only where it beats GSAP; consider light SVG/Canvas for diagrams. No 3D/WebGL unless it earns its weight.

L · Performance

Fast is a feature

Static generation, image pipeline (AVIF/WebP), lazy media, code-split motion, poster-first video, budgets on JS & LCP. Target strong Core Web Vitals on mobile.

M · SEO

Technical + content

Per-page metadata, JSON-LD (Org + CreativeWork), sitemap/robots, semantic headings, real project pages as indexable proof. Keep the groundwork already in place.
